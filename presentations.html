The Presentation Archive

A slide-by-slide visual deconstruction of CRG's June 29, 2026 pitch deck—comparing their polished slide graphics directly against reality.

Note from the Community: Below are the key slides presented by CRG executives Bob Clark, Chris McKee, and Natasha Das during their June 29, 2026 town hall, broken down with our transparent annotations.
The Conceptual Plan Slides 6–7
Figure: 600 MW / 4 Buildings / 1,200ft Buffer Layout[cite: 1]
What CRG Claimed Designed for ~600 megawatts (40% smaller than the original 1-gigawatt plan) with a minimum 1,200-foot buffer to the nearest home[cite: 1].
The Reality Check The 1-gigawatt monstrosity was a classic anchor-drop strategy. Propose an apocalyptic grid-destroyer first so that a 600-megawatt industrial park looks like a generous compromise.
A Plan Refined Through Community Feedback Slide 8
Figure: 1GW / 12 Buildings vs 600MW / 4 Buildings[cite: 1]
What CRG Claimed "We listened to the community and significantly scaled down the project footprint[cite: 1]."
The Reality Check They "listened" only after 100% of the pro-development city council was swept away in an election landslide and thousands signed recall petitions.
Environmental Stewardship - Forest Preservation Slide 9
Figure: 52% of Land Preserved (188 Acres)[cite: 1]
What CRG Claimed "We are leaving ± 188 acres, or more than 52% of the land, completely preserved, protecting natural beauty and local gravesites[cite: 1]."
The Reality Check Celebrating that they aren't paving over *every single square inch* of the property doesn't change the fact that the remaining 48% will host a massive high-security industrial campus humming 24/7.
Original vs. New Commitments to Festus Slide 24
Figure: Summary graphic of financial promises[cite: 1]
What CRG Claimed $40M in community benefits, $3M residential rate relief if usage tops 1GW, voluntary home buyouts at FMV + 15%, and a $10M decommissioning bond[cite: 1].
The Reality Check A $10M decommissioning bond is a fraction of what it costs to clean up an abandoned data center wasteland, and token rate payouts don't fix long-term grid strain.
